One capital.
Three choices.
Which one?

Container shipping, integrated logistics or port terminals — three very different bets on the same industry. The next slides break down how I think about them.

A — Container Shipping B — Logistics C — Terminals

Which one would you choose?

Know the industry before you choose

Three ways to think about each vertical. Volatility, risk and potential.

A · Container Shipping
$9T
Global trade moved by sea annually
VolatilityHIGH
Freight rates can move sharply in a short period. The cycle turns fast and earnings follow.
RiskHIGH
Heavy capex, fixed costs and oversupply can hurt returns for years if timing is wrong.
PotentialHIGH
When the market tightens, the upside is huge. Few maritime segments can rerate this quickly.
B · Logistics & Services
$1.1T
Global 3PL market · 6–7% CAGR
VolatilityLOW
Contracts and service diversification make revenue more stable than shipping.
RiskMED
The downside is usually margin pressure, competition and slower growth rather than collapse.
PotentialMED
It can compound well over time, especially with scale, but the upside is less explosive.
C · Port Terminals
20–40y
Typical concession contract length
VolatilityLOW
Terminal income is usually more stable because volumes matter more than freight rate swings.
RiskLOW
The model is more predictable, though political, regulatory and concession risk still matter.
PotentialMED
The upside is usually slower, but steady expansion, automation and long contracts can compound well.
My view

My experience has been across the maritime sector and the logistics chain, from ships to terminals and infrastructure.

That is why my view is transversal — and why this is not an easy choice.

It is a tough choice.
But I would still choose terminals.

All three verticals have good things and real risks. But if I had to put capital somewhere today, I would go for the terminal vertical: less volatility, lower risk and a business that is much harder to replicate quickly.

Why

Returns are usually more stable and less exposed to freight market swings.

Also

A good terminal takes time, permits, capital and the right location. That matters.
