Investor Takeaway

Key Outputs: What the Numbers Tell Us

Ocean

More volume, less money.

A 14% drop in freight rates, just USD 346 per FFE, erased nearly USD 1bn in EBIT. Volumes grew 9.3% and fleet utilisation hit 96%. None of it was enough. Shipping is a commodity business and when pricing power disappears, operational efficiency cannot compensate.

-USD 192m
EBIT · Largest revenue segment in the group
Logistics

Eight quarters, eight improvements.

Consistent margin expansion for two full years running. Revenue up 8.7%, EBIT up 20%, air freight volumes surging 20%. Built on contracts and value-added services, not on spot rate exposure. The integration strategy is paying off steadily.

4.6% margin
EBIT · 8th consecutive year-on-year improvement
Terminals

The toll road of the sea.

Smallest revenue contributor, highest margin by a distance. Concession contracts, high barriers to entry and pricing power independent of the freight cycle. Revenue per move grew 2.2% even through geopolitical disruption. The asset does not move, and that is precisely why it compounds.

33.2% margin
EBIT · ROIC 15.7%

Maersk full-year 2026 EBIT guidance spans from -USD 1.5bn to +USD 1.0bn. The entire range depends on a single variable, the reopening of the Red Sea and the Strait of Hormuz. The most integrated shipping group in the world has a USD 2.5bn outcome sensitivity tied to two straits staying open.
